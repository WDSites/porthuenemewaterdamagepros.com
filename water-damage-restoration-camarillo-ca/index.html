Camarillo plumbing and roof leaks start with the clock

Camarillo counted about 70,700 people in 2020 and sits at roughly 177 feet above sea level in Pleasant Valley, inside Ventura County. That elevation is much higher than the beach, so the sea is not the threat. The usual calls here come from burst pipes, water heaters, roof leaks and condensate lines.

Whatever the cause, the next steps are the same: close the shut-off, lift belongings, keep clear of outlets and call the 24/7 line. The drive starts in Port Hueneme, and a free crew can reach you the same day. For burst pipes, see emergency water removal.

Why Camarillo homes lose water differently

Owners live in about 66 percent of Camarillo housing units, based on 2020 census counts, so most claims run through a homeowner's policy. That also means long-time homeowners may have pipes and fixtures of the same age. A supply line that has been in the wall for decades fails without warning, often at night when the pressure rises.

Camarillo's city water comes from two sources. The city says it imports about 60 percent of its supply through Calleguas Municipal Water District and pumps about 40 percent from wells. The North Pleasant Valley Desalter began supplying drinking water in January 2023. Hardness can vary with the mix, which is why mineral scale can build up in older fittings.

Before we arrive in Camarillo

Stop the water

Find the shut-off at the fixture or the main valve near the house. If you cannot find it, check your water bill for the number of the provider.

Power down safely

Flip a wet room's breaker only if you can get to the panel without stepping in water. Never touch outlets or switches near standing water.

Move what you can

Lift furniture, take rugs outside to dry, and move papers and electronics to a higher shelf.

Photograph and call

Photograph the water and the damaged items, then call the 24/7 line.

Camarillo facts that affect water damage

Camarillo is on the Calleguas Creek watershed, and the city says about two-thirds of residents are served by the city water system while Camrosa serves most of the rest, including Santa Rosa Valley. Knowing your provider helps if a main line leaks before the meter.

Most of Camarillo's rain falls from December through February, based on the area's climate normals. Roof flashing and gutters tend to fail first when the first big storm comes after a dry summer, so check roofs before the rainy season. Ask the city building department about permit rules for roof and wall repairs.
